Abstract

As machine learning systems grow more complex, an increasing share of the work of designing, training, evaluating, and maintaining them is itself being carried out by learning algorithms and generative agents. We survey this practice under the name AI for AI (AI4AI): the study of algorithms, systems, and evaluation protocols in which one AI component—a searcher, meta-learner, generative model, multi-agent population, or automated evaluator—materially improves another AI artifact or process across the model lifecycle. The framing is broader than classical AutoML, which historically emphasized pipeline configuration and architecture search, and narrower than “AI for X”, where the object of improvement lies outside AI systems.

AI4AI is not only a byproduct of large language models. We reconstruct a cumulative lineage in which early agents and reinforcement learning made improvement endogenous, AutoML and meta-learning made outer loops explicit, self-play supplied endogenous curricula, and language-model agents widened the action space to code, tools, and repositories. Building on this lineage, we propose a four-axis taxonomy organized by lifecycle stage, target of improvement, degree of automation, and method family, and close with open challenges—evaluator circularity, cost asymmetry, reproducibility, and safety under recursion—together with a staged roadmap toward measurement-first benchmarks, hybrid oversight, and auditable closed loops.

Contributions

Four moves that turn a scattered literature into one measurable family.

01 · History

A convergence, not a break

Reconstructs AI4AI as a cumulative lineage: agents and RL, AutoML and meta-learning, self-play, then LLM agents. Recursive self-improvement is the high-automation end of the same spectrum.

02 · Taxonomy

Four shared coordinates

A coordinate system for comparison: lifecycle stage, target of improvement, degree of automation, and method family. One cell per axis, or a trajectory across cells over time.

03 · Mechanisms

Nested objectives, made explicit

Landmark methods reviewed at mechanism level: what the outer loop proposes, what the inner loop scores, which artifact changes, and whether the proposer can itself be rewritten.

04 · Roadmap

Measurement before autonomy

Open challenges (evaluator circularity, cost accounting, reproducibility, safety under recursion) mapped to a staged agenda: external evaluators first, hybrid oversight, auditable closed loops.

A compressed lineage

Each stage removed an automation bottleneck and introduced a new evaluation problem. See Section 2 for the full account.
