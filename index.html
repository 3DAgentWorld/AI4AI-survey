The Review in two paragraphs. Read the full text for the complete version.

Abstract

Foundation models increasingly help build other AI systems. They generate training data, provide feedback and write programs that guide learning. This Review examines how foundation models and agents built on them improve models and agent systems. We focus on cases where the improver itself changes. We compare updates to experience generators, evaluation mechanisms and programs that produce improvements. A system may combine these paths. Their effects depend on the feedback used, the experience kept for later rounds and the external checks that remain fixed.

We examine their benefits, failure modes and costs. We distinguish changes to an improver from gains in its ability to improve AI in later rounds. This distinction guides future research on independent tests, fair budget comparisons, use on new tasks and progress over repeated updates.

Contributions

Four moves that turn a scattered literature into one testable question.

01 · Anatomy

Three improver roles

Methods grouped by the work the improver supplies: training experience, evaluation feedback, or changes to prompts, workflows and code. One system may take several roles, and the improver and improvee need not share a form.

02 · Change

When the improver updates

The paths by which generators, evaluators and modification programs themselves change, and what each path keeps for later rounds: selected traces, stored feedback, rubrics, reward code or archived agents.

03 · Limits

Gains with their failure modes

Coverage narrowing under success-based selection, evaluator drift, error accumulation in memory and archives, and the transfer and cost questions that task scores alone cannot answer.

04 · Evidence

Tests that separate causes

A comparison protocol for updated improvers: two copies of the same improvee, one changed by the updated improver and one by a fixed earlier version, under comparable budgets and independent tests.
